UEi Brand System

Built a complete brand identity and governance framework from scratch — logo to collateral, across every touchpoint.

The Problem

No rules. No consistency. No brand.

When I joined UEi Test Instruments, there was no formal brand system in place. The existing visual identity was inconsistent and confusing — internally and externally. Different teams applied the logo differently. Colors varied across materials. Typography was uncontrolled. Packaging, marketing collateral, product faceplates, and digital materials each looked like they came from a different company.

There was no single source of truth. No documentation. No rules anyone could follow. As the sole creative resource, I identified this as the foundational problem — one that was quietly undermining every piece of communication the company produced. Before anything else could be fixed, the brand itself needed to be defined.

The Deliverables

A complete brand governance system — built alone, from zero.

I created the full UEi Brand Standards document — a living reference guide covering every aspect of the brand, designed to be used by internal teams and external partners alike. This wasn't a mood board or a style tile. It was a production-ready governance framework with precise specifications for every use case.

The Process

Audit everything. Define the rules. Document them permanently.

Building a brand system from scratch with no predecessor means starting from observation — looking at what existed, identifying every inconsistency, and making deliberate decisions about what the brand should be. Every rule I wrote had to be specific enough to prevent misuse and flexible enough to work across a 200+ SKU product line.

1
Brand audit
Collected every existing piece of branded material — packaging, product sheets, ads, web pages, manuals — and mapped every inconsistency. This became the brief: a complete list of everything the brand system needed to solve.
2
Logo system definition
Defined three logo types — outlined color, 3D color, and solid — with precise color schemes for each, quiet area specifications based on the segmented bar in the logo, minimum display sizes (0.875" with byline), and a full misuse guide covering incorrect coloration, distortion, and unauthorized typeface substitution.
3
Color and typography standards
Established the full color palette with exact CMYK and Pantone values for primary colors, secondary colors, and product housing. Selected Univers STD as the brand typeface family — documented all weights, use cases, and size hierarchies for every communication format.
4
Packaging construction standards
Documented the exact construction rules for every face of UEi packaging — logo placement relative to hangholes, color blocking proportions, red gradient specifications, black steel bar dimensions, feature listing format, bilingual content (English, French, Spanish), and safety certification placement.
5
Collateral template system
Built a complete library of collateral standards — 7 flyer template variations, 4 product sheet formats (letter, tabloid, half-fold, tri-fold), 2 manual formats (sheet and book), web page examples, advertisement standards, PowerPoint slide templates, and letterhead specifications.
6
Documentation and distribution
Compiled everything into a versioned Brand Standards document (Version 3.0) — a production-ready reference used by internal teams and external partners for all brand applications. Updated and maintained across multiple versions as the brand evolved.

The System in Detail

Every touchpoint. Every rule. Documented.

The brand system extended beyond the logo into every communication format UEi produced — from retail packaging to product manuals to digital advertising. Each section of the standards document gave teams exact specifications, not general direction. The goal was to make brand compliance the path of least resistance.

Left: Flyer, product sheet, and manual standards. Right: Packaging construction rules and collateral templates.

The Result

One system. Every team. Every touchpoint.

The Brand Standards document became the single source of truth for UEi's visual identity — used by internal marketing, product, and operations teams as well as external print vendors, packaging suppliers, and agency partners. For the first time, everyone working on UEi materials was working from the same rulebook.

Brand consistency across packaging, print, digital, and product improved immediately. New products could be onboarded into the system without starting from scratch. External partners could execute brand-compliant work without back-and-forth. The system I built is still the foundation UEi operates from today.
