Product Packaging System

Structural redesign that cut shipping costs and unified brand presence across an entire product line.

The Problem

Three problems. One packaging system to solve them all.

UEi's original packaging was a blister card format — a plastic shell heat-sealed to a printed cardboard backing. It was the industry default, and it was holding the brand back in three measurable ways.

First, shipping inefficiency: the bulky blister format meant only 20 units fit per master carton. That drove up freight costs across every shipment, for every SKU, every order cycle. Second, weak shelf presence: blister cards look commodity. On a retail pegboard next to competitors, nothing about the packaging said UEi was a premium, trusted brand. Third, no brand consistency: with no unified packaging architecture, each product looked like it came from a different company. There was no visual system — just a collection of individual packages.

Original blister card format — DL369 Digital Clamp-On Multimeter

The Process

From dieline to retail shelf — full ownership.

I owned every layer of this project — structural design, dieline creation, and all artwork files. No outside vendors, no structural engineer handoff. That end-to-end ownership meant faster iteration and tighter control over the final result.

1
Structural audit
Measured existing product dimensions across the line to determine the optimal new box format — one that would maximize units per carton while maintaining retail hang-tab functionality.
2
Dieline design
Designed the structural dieline from scratch — cut lines, fold lines, bleed zones, and hang-tab slot — built as a reusable template that could scale across the entire product line.
3
Brand system application
Developed a consistent artwork framework — UEi logo placement, color blocking, typography hierarchy, product photography treatment, and feature callout system — designed to work across every SKU regardless of product size or category.
4
Production artwork
Built print-ready files for all faces of the box — front, back, sides, and top — with complete bilingual content (English and Spanish) and all required compliance markings, certifications, and regulatory symbols.
5
Full line rollout
Applied the system across the entire UEi product line — every SKU, every category, every package size — creating a unified brand presence across the complete retail assortment.

Structural dieline (left) and full flat artwork (right) — both designed and produced in-house

The Result

A 75% shipping efficiency gain — and a brand that finally looked like one.

The new hang-tab box format fit 35 units per master carton, up from 20 — a 75% increase in units per shipment. That's a direct, recurring cost reduction on every freight order across the full product line.

On the retail shelf, the impact was immediate. A consistent brand architecture — unified color blocking, logo treatment, typography, and product photography style — made UEi's full assortment look intentional and premium for the first time.
